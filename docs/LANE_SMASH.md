# Lane Smash

A resistance-controlled 3D cycling game built inside OpenRide. Original code, graphics, courses and optional electronic music; no Peloton subscription or network connection is needed by the game. Lane Smash is the only available experience. The app opens directly into its track selector while preserving the installed package and saved rides.

## Start a ride

1. Open **Lane Smash**. Its track selector appears directly; no profile, FTP, or game-selection screen is required.
2. Choose **10, 15, 20 or 30 min**. Each duration has 25 named tracks.
3. Select a track, or tap **Generate random track**. Drag anywhere on the library, including on the cards themselves, to scroll it; a tap that does not move picks the card. There are no visible scrollbars.
4. Choose the resistance difficulty. **Kid** starts at 0 and uses half-size lane steps; its full range is 0–20. **Last** and **Best** show scores for this exact track and difficulty; incomplete last attempts are marked **partial**.
5. Pedal until **Bike connected · ready** appears, then tap **Start ride**.

Every track has one scoring lane at a time. Turn the physical knob to reach it. Difficulty sets the resistance at both ends of the six-lane road, without changing the selected track layout.

Resistance moves the motorcycle smoothly within each lane; crossing that lane's resistance limit shifts it into the next one. A pale blue road highlight shows the target lane. **Move Left / Less Resistance** or **Move Right / More Resistance** appears when you need to turn the knob and fades once you are inside the correct range. Bright violet floors with white start/end lines mark Power Bursts; pedal harder through them to build their charge.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kid | 0–3 | 4–6 | 7–10 | 11–13 | 14–17 | 18–20 |
| Light | 15–21 | 22–28 | 29–35 | 36–42 | 43–49 | 50–56 |
| Moderate | 20–26 | 27–33 | 34–40 | 41–47 | 48–54 | 55–61 |

The five adult ranges match the published Lanebreak Bike update, and the seven-value lanes match every individual Lanebreak lane we could find, including the lane-edge numbers visible in review footage of the game. See [range research](research/lanebreak/06-difficulty-ranges.md). Kid is our own extra-easy resistance option: the ordinary seven-point lane step becomes 3.5, with whole-number readings split into alternating four- and three-value bands. It uses the same tracks, cadence targets and scoring, with its own last/best scores and ghost. Values outside the chosen range do not score. A lane must settle for 150 ms. The app never turns the physical resistance knob for you; the roller-coaster route is visual and independent of resistance.

## Points and movement

- **Gold bars:** pedal in the target lane. Points accumulate with distance through the marked section.
- **Mint arrows:** stay in the target lane and between the displayed minimum and maximum rpm. Both endpoints count. A soft mint gradient stays attached to the section beneath the raised arrows.
- **Machine-gun fire:** every mint-arrow section is strafed by a raider on a hover sled. Two lines of fire rake the road, one ahead of you and one behind; hold the displayed rpm range and you ride in the gap. Speed up toward the top of the range and the line ahead closes in; slow toward the bottom and the line behind does. Outside the range the fire reaches you: after a two-second warning at the start of the section, a round lands after 0.6 seconds outside and then every half second, each costing up to **60 points**, at most 15 rounds (900 points) per section. Your lane does not matter for this; only your pedalling speed does.
- **The cadence gauge and its sweet spot:** shortly before a mint-arrow section, a gauge appears beside your motorcycle. Its track runs from the section's lowest rpm at the bottom to its highest at the top; the marker and the big number are your cadence. Keep the marker on the track to score. The bright band across the middle is the **sweet spot** (the middle of the range, 3 rpm either side): ride there and the section pays **20% more**. The gauge turns white when you are in it, says AIM FOR MIDDLE when you are in range but outside it, and turns red with FASTER or SLOWER when you are out of range. The bonus is extra score only; it never affects your stars.
- **Violet power sections:** accumulate output in the target lane. When you finish one, the points it earned burst onto the middle of the screen with a word for how hard you pushed (FULL POWER at the 150 W reference, SUPERCHARGED and OVERCHARGED above it). The floor charges and brightens as energy builds, with faster pulses at higher cadence and a growing triangular finish gate. Current source awards eighteen points per five speed-weighted joules, multiplying output by (rpm / 90)² during the section. This makes complete-burst rewards proportional to speed at equal watts; 90 rpm retains the prior energy reward. There is no maximum-cadence target or power-score ceiling.
- Bars and arrows sit on the road without hovering; adjacent sections leave a clear space between the final target and the next section’s first target. Gold bars, arrows and the power section’s finish gate shatter when the front wheel touches them. The visual collision does not award points by itself: the correct lane, cadence and output rules still apply. Objects in other lanes remain intact.
- **Red bombs:** frequent rows occupy all five lanes outside the highlighted scoring lane. They start shortly after a section begins and repeat through Collect, Pedal speed and Power Burst sections. All lane-change gaps, the target lane and cooldown stay clear. Hitting one removes up to **1,800 points**, shows the amount lost in red and triggers a flash, shockwave, debris and sound. Your score cannot fall below zero, and avoiding these static bombs gives no bonus.
- **Incoming Fire:** attackers launch two shots toward marked blast zones. Move into the highlighted lane during the nine-second preparation, then follow **Pedal Faster** or **Pedal Slower**. Attack lanes vary through lanes 3–6. A normal shot charges for two seconds and flies for nine; entering a firing boundary or pending blast zone in the wrong lane triggers a rapid drop within 450 ms. A hit costs up to **1,800 points**. Passing completely beyond the marked zone awards **1,800 immediately**, without waiting for the bomb to land. Safely staying behind earns **zero**, with no penalty. Changing lanes alone does not evade a normal full-width blast. Play returns shortly after the second impact fades.
- Stars rate how well you met the targets encountered during your ride. Complete the full selected duration and reach 25%, 60% or 90% for one, two or three stars. Extra burst output cannot replace missed lane/cadence targets. Road bombs lower your points but do not directly change the star percentage. Incoming Fire adds 1,800 available target-credit points per shot; either safe dodge earns that credit. Following Pedal Slower therefore does not hurt your stars even though it earns no score bonus.

A representative simulated 20-minute Challenging ride across all 25 tracks is recorded in the validation notes for each revision (about 173,000 points in revision 12; revision 13 put gaps between sections and added the sweet-spot bonus, so the figure moved again). Your actual total depends on duration, cadence, measured power and accuracy; it is not normalized to a fixed result. Older saved courses keep their original point values.

Tracks warm up in lanes 1–2, then work mainly in lanes 3–6, with shuffled waves that visit each work lane. No required change exceeds three lanes. The final minute requests a low-lane cooldown; current source finishes an active section or delivers content in an already-requested lane before stepping down. **Collect and Pedal speed sections last at least ten seconds at maximum game speed**, longer when pedalling more slowly. Power Bursts vary between five and seven seconds at maximum speed. Clear approaches give you time to adjust resistance; shorter gaps separate sections that remain in the same lane. A lane stays highlighted throughout.

Work cadence targets decrease every two lanes: **85–105 rpm in lanes 1–2, 70–90 in lanes 3–4, and 55–75 in lanes 5–6**. Warm-up uses **75–95**. Current work courses use lanes 3–6; the lower-lane work band remains defined for consistency. These are Lane Smash's chosen bands, informed by Lanebreak footage. Difficulty resistance ranges are unchanged.

Incoming Fire begins around 75 seconds and repeats about every three minutes, honoring an already-announced approach and waiting for its full section to finish; attacks that would intrude on the last minute are omitted. Full sections resume after each attack. Only the fixed ride finish can truncate an active section in build 32; cooldown finishes an active section before stepping down. Each section's last target shows its earned total at the endpoint; this is a recap, not a second bonus. Exact timings, spacing and units are in the [parameter reference](lane-smash/PARAMETERS.md).

You ride a long, low motorcycle with glowing wheels, twin drive rotors and thrusters. The latest source gives it a rounded superbike rear tyre four times its former width, with a matching wider rim and swingarm clearance; the front stays slim. Everything on it follows your pedalling: the rotors turn once per pedal revolution, the wheels roll at game speed, the thrusters lengthen with effort, and the bike leans into lane changes. Faster pedalling moves farther down the track, and the road rushes past to match: the speed shown is your cadence × 0.8 in mph, so about **48 mph at 60 rpm, 77 mph at 96 rpm and 112 mph flat out at 140 rpm**. Ease right off and it slows with you; stop and it stops. Higher cadence cannot overrun the course. This is a game-speed scale, not a road-bike speed estimate. The 10/15/20/30-minute roads are 19.2/28.5/37.8/56.5 miles long on screen, including a buffer beyond the maximum reachable distance. Each ride still ends at the selected active time.

The old **Reference power** control is gone. An internal 150 W reference supplies the star target for power sections; it does not alter resistance, measured watts or the points you can earn.

## Ghost, scenery and scores

**Settings & history** offers **Music** (with the track picker and seek slider), **Sound effects**, **Best-ride ghost**, **Background motion**, **Graphics**, and the latest 50 attempts. Selecting a saved attempt replays its stored course, including generated tracks. Track-level last/best statistics remain available even when an attempt is older than the history list.

The ghost is your highest-scoring completed ride on the same exact course and resistance difficulty. It appears as a see-through projection of your motorcycle, following the recorded distance and lane at the same elapsed time. It fades when overlapping you or becoming too distant. **BEST RIDE** keeps showing how many feet it is ahead/behind, plus your points difference, even when the bike is out of view. Partial attempts cannot replace that best.

Eight immediately available palettes are assigned to named tracks: **Arctic, Cobalt, Copper, Titanium, Petrol, Amber, Ember and Glacier**. Four flowing psychedelic backgrounds replace the floating scenery: **Prismatic flow, Kaleidoscope, Liquid aurora and Vortex**. Cadence controls flow speed; resistance smoothly changes the pattern and colors. Random generation varies both palette and environment; saved replays retain the exact combination. There are no cosmetic unlocks or rider trails.

Music starts when the app opens, on a random track at a random point, and keeps playing through the selector, rides and results; when a track ends another random one begins. It never cuts in or out: starting, stopping, changing track and skipping all fade. Use **Settings & history** in the main menu or **Audio** while riding to pick a track, drag the slider to skip backward or forward, and turn **Music** and **Sound effects** on or off independently. The switches persist until changed from either location, including after restart. The three tracks are Discovery Project (Electric Forest), DNS Mix and Dystopian Euphoria by Devloe; a build without them plays the original **Night Drive** loop (128 BPM). Effects include soft contact notes, section chords, power charging, the engine, lane changes, warnings, launches, machine-gun fire and impacts. By default the effects sit a little under the music (about 2 dB), so cues stay clear without talking over the soundtrack.

The whole picture moves with the music. In the background, bass makes the tunnel breathe, thickens its filaments and warms their colour; mids bend its shape and slide it between its two colours; treble adds sparkle and cools the highlights; each kick sends rings of light down it. On the road, **a pulse of light leaves your bike on every beat** and runs ahead down the lane lines as a chevron, the lane lines themselves take their brightness and colour from the spectrum (bass in the middle of the road, treble at the edges), the rails and their curtains of light follow the treble and mids, and the arches over the road take turns with bass, mids and treble. In the interface, the accent lines breathe the same way: the panel edges, the track chip (which carries a small eight-bar meter of the music), your lane's outline and the progress line in the ride, and the wordmark, header line, Start button and a meter beside **Now playing** in the menu. Text never changes colour. Everything is eased so it swells and settles instead of flickering. Your pedalling still sets how fast you travel. With Music off the picture is calm and the road keeps a soft, steady pulse.

**Graphics** is **Auto** by default: the game lowers the 3D detail by itself if the tablet cannot hold a smooth frame rate over several seconds (a single stutter no longer counts), and remembers that. The 3D picture is drawn larger than before and its edges are smoothed, so the motorcycle and the lines of the road no longer look stepped. **High** and **Performance** force a setting. Every event plays when enabled, with no repeat cooldown. All gameplay instructions remain visual. **Reduced** background motion dims and softens the effect, uses gentle curves without inversions, and removes the camera knock, the red edge pulse on hits and the beat-driven pulses. Normal motion includes vertical loops, full rolls and twisting climbs, independent of lane resistance. The slightly transparent road is a fixed 3D course: the camera travels along its curves instead of reshaping the road toward a moving endpoint. Lane lines and scoring objects stay clear while the background shows through the surface. Each named track has a repeatable route; generated tracks get their own seeded curves, loops and twists. New ride snapshots save that route. The psychedelic opening follows the view down the real course, with longer, smoother transitions. Its patterns now flow outward and past you using the same distance as the road: pedal faster to move through them faster, and stop to halt forward motion. Track cards preview their own colors and animated patterns behind a dark text gradient; Reduced motion freezes those previews.

## Uninterrupted rides and saved data

There is **no Pause option**. Finish the selected duration or choose **End Ride** and confirm. The timer continues while the end confirmation is open and while you stop pedalling. Below 20 rpm, ordinary road targets award no points; an incoming shot still resolves at its scheduled impact. Sensor loss, invalid samples, a lost input batch or a display stall of more than a second freeze scoring so missing data cannot award points. A shorter stutter is simply caught up and no longer brings up the interruption screen (until revision 13 anything over a quarter of a second did, which is why that screen could flash up and vanish during a ride on the Bike). One second of healthy input triggers automatic recovery followed by a three-second unscored countdown; there is no manual Resume button.

The total score and power-section points follow new values continuously, counting quickly and easing into the exact result without stopping and restarting each second. Penalties ease downward promptly. Captions and panels stay still; speed, ghost gap and ride-metric numbers keep their one-second refresh. Section instructions change immediately at a boundary. Prompts use **Pedal Faster**, **Pedal Slower**, **Stay in This Lane**, and the direction cues **Move Left / Less Resistance** or **Move Right / More Resistance**. The lane dock shows the resistance ranges.

Attempts save before play, every five active seconds, on interruption and before results. Closing the app leaves an **Interrupted** attempt instead of resuming a partial course. Storage failures offer **Retry save** or ending the ride.

Return through recent apps or the **Lane Smash** app icon after switching away. Both lead to the same persistent game activity. **Exit** returns to the tablet launcher.

Backups include course snapshots, scores and completed ghost traces. Settings such as music are local preferences. Installed build 32 uses **course 13 / generator 10 / scoring 12** as described below; build-31 snapshots retain course 12 / generator 9 / scoring 11; build-28 snapshots retain course 11 / generator 8 / scoring 10. Older snapshots retain their original scoring and comparison identity, so older history remains readable while changed layouts have separate last/best comparisons. The wider rear tyre is a visual-only update and does not reset comparisons. Room remains at schema 9.

## Revision 21 changes (installed in build 29)

Revision 21 moves the bomb meter beside the bike: green above the red blast zone means a predicted safe pass, dark slate below means avoiding it without points. The marker follows your projected position at impact. Inner lane lines acquire a subtle red tint when resistance is near that boundary; moving toward the middle clears it.

Approaches are labeled **UP NEXT**, active sections **NOW**. The cadence meter and shooter share a short 1.2-second preview; firing begins with the actual section. An announced lane gets its section before an attack takes over. Cooldown finishes a section already underway and honors an already-requested lane before stepping down, so it can start slightly later than the one-minute mark.

Power Bursts reward speed as well as output. At the same watts, a complete burst at 120 rpm earns roughly twice as much as at 60 rpm. At 90 rpm the prior energy reward is unchanged. Course 12 / generator 9 / scoring 11 keeps these scores separate from older bests and ghosts; previous ride history remains readable. These changes remain included in **build 32** on the Peloton. See [revision-21 validation](specs/lane-smash-v21.md) and the [installation record](specs/lane-smash-v21-installation.md).

## Easier Kid cadence (introduced in build 30)

New Kid rides halve the Pedal Speed targets, rounded to whole rpm: **38–48** during warm-up, then **43–53 / 35–45 / 28–38** across the three lane pairs. The meter, points and gunfire agree on the lower speeds. Adult targets are unchanged. Older saved Kid replays retain their original targets; new Kid last/best/ghost comparisons are separate. At these lower cadences, the same road sections take longer to travel through.

## Installing the fork on the Bike

This is an app installation, not a replacement operating system. The original Bike's non-rotating screen does not identify its tablet generation. This build needs **Android 10 or later** and an ARM64 tablet. Sustained riding performance must be checked on the actual tablet.

**First installation update, November 13, 2026:** code 20 was installed and launched on the owner's PLTN-RB1VQ, Android 11 / RQ.260424.A, ARM64. The selector displayed **Bike connected · ready**. Live pedalling and sustained ride performance remain untested; see the [device installation record](research/lanebreak/08-first-install-followup.md).

1. On the Bike, check **Settings → Device Settings → About tablet** for the model, Android version and build. Firmware may arrange these labels differently. Do not start a firmware update just to inspect them.
2. On the Mac, open [OpenPelo](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/releases). Follow its **Developer Mode Guide** for that tablet, enable **USB debugging**, and connect a USB data cable to the tablet's data port. Leave the Bike's sensor cable connected.
3. Accept **Allow USB debugging** on the tablet. In OpenPelo, choose the Bike under **Target Device**; use **Refresh Devices** if needed.
4. Choose **Install local APK**, then select the signed `openride-neon-<version code>.apk` built from this fork. The normal **Compatible applications → OpenRide** catalog entry installs upstream, not this game build.
5. In OpenPelo, open **Tools → Installed App Manager**, search for **Lane Smash**, and click **Launch**. The Bike should show the track selector. Pedal until it reports ready, start a 10-minute Light ride, and verify that the top-left rpm, W and resistance values respond to pedalling and the knob.
6. Complete a 10-minute ride, then reopen the app to confirm its saved score and ghost. Test End Ride on a separate attempt. Follow with a full ride before relying on it for daily use.

For daily access without the Mac, a tablet launcher such as **Lawnchair** can display the Lane Smash app icon. OpenPelo offers it under **Compatible applications → Launchers**; after installation, use **Tools → Set Default Launcher → Lawnchair → Set Default**. This changes the home screen to the launcher, not directly to Lane Smash. The current signed build has its HOME alias disabled and no accessible control to enable it; the removed upstream Profile setting is not available. Direct automatic startup requires an app change or a separately verified device-specific setup. Do not use the old debug-only `run-as` instructions with this non-debuggable installer.

OpenPelo's generic Developer Mode Guide includes **Firmware Reset**. First confirm the tablet model, Android version and build, and try existing developer access. A reset is not established as necessary for this user's tablet; if USB debugging is missing or blocked, stop before resetting and obtain instructions for that exact firmware. Leave the Bike sensor cable connected.

The fork package is `dev.digitalducktape.openride.neon`, with real sensors enabled, a dedicated signing key and a `kbokarius/OpenRide` update channel. It can coexist with upstream OpenRide. Updating this package does not reset the tablet, disable Peloton apps, change the home app or cancel a membership.

For later updates, keep the same package and signing key. Install a newer signed APK using **Install local APK**. The former Profile and App updates controls are removed. The latest signed installer and verified Bike installation is **code 32 / revision 24**, adding off-lane bombs between sections with protected lane-change corridors, while retaining the faster resistance feedback and previous gameplay fixes. It was installed in place over Wi-Fi on November 18, 2026, then opened with **Bike connected · ready**. See [current source/build/device status](lane-smash/PARAMETERS.md#units-and-release-state). No fork release is automatically published by a local build.

Wi-Fi updates work when the laptop and Bike share a local network and the Bike has wireless debugging enabled. This laptop's existing authorization was sufficient; no new pairing code or USB connection was required for build 28. Once OpenPelo shows the connected Bike under **Target Device**, use **Install local APK** as above. The [revision-20 installation record](specs/lane-smash-v20.md#repeating-a-wi-fi-update) contains the verified ADB command workflow and first-time pairing distinction. Check that no ride is active before installing.

Update this fork in place: do not uninstall it or clear its app data, since that can remove locally stored history. Automatic backups remain enabled, but the former manual Profile controls are removed. Do not uninstall upstream to resolve a signature mismatch. The dedicated fork avoids that conflict. More firmware-specific context and sources: [installation research](research/lanebreak/04-installation.md).

## Building and maintaining

- Godot editor: **4.7.2**, matching the Android engine dependency.
- Java: **17+**; the local verification used Android Studio's bundled JBR.
- Set `ANDROID_HOME` to the SDK and `GODOT_BIN` to the Godot executable.
- `./gradlew :app:testDebugUnitTest` runs Android unit tests.
- `./gradlew :app:assembleDebug` builds the simulated-sensor app for local Android testing.
- `./gradlew :app:assembleNeonReal` builds the ARM64 real-sensor fork. Set `NEON_SIGNING_PROPERTIES` to a private properties file containing `storeFile`, `storePassword`, `keyAlias` and `keyPassword`. Without it the APK is deliberately unsigned. Never commit signing keys or passwords; back them up securely and reuse them for updates.
- Godot tests, captures and full-course simulation commands are recorded in the validation report and [framework documentation](GAMES.md).
- `LaneSmashCatalog.gd` defines the named/seeded courses. Legacy JSON remains exported for saved replays. Keep the generator version and course hash stable unless deliberately versioning content.
- `godot --path games -s res://tests/demo_lane_smash.gd` opens an automated desktop ride with an isolated completed ghost. It uses simulated sensor values and separate demonstration history.

Lane Smash is the only selectable game; other game assets are excluded from the APK. See the [structured architecture and rules](lane-smash/README.md), [current parameters](lane-smash/PARAMETERS.md) and [revision 19 changes and validation](specs/lane-smash-v19.md).

## Resistance response update (introduced in build 31 / revision 23)

Previous build 30 requested sensor callbacks every 200 ms and could display resistance/steering text up to one second late. Build 31 onward requests 100 ms, shows changed resistance on the next rendered frame, makes steering text react immediately and speeds up the motorcycle's smooth response. The red warning on an interior lane line is brighter. Scoring still uses the same raw readings and 150 ms lane settling; the firmware determines when fresh measurements are available. See the [audit and validation](specs/lane-smash-v23.md).


## Bombs between sections (installed in build 32 / revision 24)

New rides also place bombs in non-target lanes between sections. When changing lanes, the old lane and the path to the highlighted lane stay clear for at least 4–6 seconds at maximum speed, depending on how many lanes you need to cross; slower riding provides more time. Then the next bomb row leaves only the target lane clear. Gaps between sections in the same lane guard the other five lanes. Incoming Fire and cooldown still suppress road mines. Penalties and effects are unchanged.

This uses course 13 / generator 10 / scoring 12, so new last/best comparisons are separate from the old layouts; historical scores and ghosts remain readable. Installed build 32 includes these guarded gaps; saved build-31 courses retain their previous mine-free gaps. See [revision 24](specs/lane-smash-v24.md) for exact parameters and validation.
